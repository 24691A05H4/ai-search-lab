Game 1 Algorithms
8-Puzzle Search Mechanics
DLS with Path Cycle Detection
  • Path-Based Cycle Detection: The 8-puzzle graph has cycles (e.g. moving tile left then right). An ancestor set on the current branch prevents infinite recursion.
  • Cutoff Definition: Any non-goal node where further expansion is prevented solely by the depth limit.
IDDFS & Manhattan Heuristic
  • Iterative Deepening (IDDFS): Runs DLS sequentially from depth 0 up to max_depth. Combines BFS completeness with DFS space efficiency O(d).
  • Manhattan Distance: Admissible & consistent heuristic guiding A* to find the optimal shortest solution path.
Game 2 Algorithms
Tic-Tac-Toe Adversarial Minimax
Minimax Decision Rule
  • Alternates between MAX (AI 'O') and MIN (Human 'X').
  • Terminal score: +(100 - depth) for AI win (incentivizes fast checkmate) and -(100 - depth) for human win.
Zero Cutoffs at Depth 9
  • At depth 9, cutoff nodes collapse to 0 because all paths terminate in a win, loss, or draw within 9 plies.
  • At shallow depths (1 to 4), heuristic evaluation drives decisions.

Branching Factor & State Space
  • 8-Puzzle: Branching factor is small: $b \in [2, 4]$ (average $b \approx 2.67$). Cyclic graph.
  • Tic-Tac-Toe: Branching factor starts high at $b=9$, decreasing by 1 at each ply ($9 \rightarrow 8 \rightarrow 7 \rightarrow \dots$). Directed acyclic tree.

Viva Defense Masterclass
Summary & Key Viva Questions
  • Q: Why does 8-Puzzle require cycle detection?
    A: Because sliding a tile is reversible. Without path-based cycle detection, DLS gets trapped in infinite ping-pong loops.
  • Q: What is inversion parity in 8-puzzle?
    A: On a 3×3 board, a state is solvable if and only if the number of inversions among tiles 1–8 is even. Shuffling from the goal state guarantees 100% solvability.
  • Q: Why does Tic-Tac-Toe have 0 cutoffs at Depth 9?
    A: Every branch terminates in a win, loss, or draw within 9 moves; the depth limit is never breached without reaching a terminal node.
