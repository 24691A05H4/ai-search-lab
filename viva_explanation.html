2. 🧩 8-Puzzle Game & AI Solver
⚙️ Working Process:
  • Interactive Sliding Board: Users click any adjacent tile to slide it into the EMPTY cell.
  • Solvability Parity Theorem: On a 3×3 grid, a puzzle state is solvable if and only if its inversion count is EVEN. Shuffling executes valid random moves from the goal state, mathematically guaranteeing 100% solvable configurations.
  • AI Solver: Employs A* Search with Manhattan Distance ($h(n) = \sum |r - r^*| + |c - c^*|$) to find the optimal shortest path to the goal with step-by-step playback.
🎯 Academic / Practical Use:

Demonstrates legal state-space transitions, inversion parity mathematical guarantees, and informed heuristic search path planning.

🗣️ Exact Speech Script for AI Mam:

In AI theory, half of all random 8-puzzle permutations are mathematically unsolvable because their inversion count is odd. Our shuffle algorithm guarantees solvability by applying legal transitions from the goal state. When the user clicks 'Solve with AI', our A* search uses the admissible Manhattan Distance heuristic to compute the shortest solution path."

💡 Golden Viva Defense Cheat Sheet (Common Professor Questions)

Q1: Why do Tic-Tac-Toe cutoffs drop to exactly 0 at Depth 9?
Answer: Because a Tic-Tac-Toe board has only 9 cells. No match can exceed 9 plies; therefore, every branch reaches a terminal state (Win, Loss, or Draw) within 9 plies and the search is never prematurely cut off.
Q2: Why is cycle detection necessary in 8-Puzzle but not in Tic-Tac-Toe?
Answer: 8-Puzzle tile sliding actions are reversible (moving left then right returns to the same state), creating cycles in the state space graph. Tic-Tac-Toe cells cannot be un-played, so empty cells strictly decrease, naturally preventing cycles.
Q3: What makes the Manhattan Distance heuristic admissible?
Answer: Manhattan Distance calculates the straight-line grid distance for each tile ignoring obstacles. Since tiles must move around other tiles, the true path length $h^*(n)$ is always greater than or equal to Manhattan Distance ($h(n) \le h^*(n)$), guaranteeing optimal A* solutions.
Q4: Why return (100 - depth) on a win in Minimax?
Answer: To force the AI to choose the fastest possible win. A win at Depth 1 gives score +99, while a win at Depth 3 gives +97. The maximizing AI prefers +99, finding the quickest checkmate.
